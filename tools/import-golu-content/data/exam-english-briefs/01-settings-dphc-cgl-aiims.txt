#DPHC-024 | word-processing and table formatting in a police office
- A table of the week's duty hours is set with fixed column widths, so that names and times line up when the sheet is printed.
- Headings are typed in bold once, and the same font size is used for every row, which keeps the printed page easy to scan.
- Before printing, the clerk uses the preview to confirm that no column has spilled onto a second page.
- The document is saved under a name that gives the subject and the date, so that it can be found again without opening other files.

#DPHC-025 | a ten-minute ministerial typing practice passage
- The practice passage mixes plain sentences with names, dates and reference numbers, because ministerial work combines all three.
- The typist reads a line ahead of the cursor, which helps keep a steady rhythm instead of stopping at every comma.
- Capital letters, full stops and hyphens are copied exactly as shown, since a missing mark changes the record.
- At the end of the practice, the typist compares the typed text with the original and notes every word that differs.

// ---------------------------------------------------------------- CGL-DEST
#CGL-DEST-001 | the monthly departmental expenditure statement
- The statement lists every head of expenditure with its budget figure, the amount spent in the month and the balance carried forward.
- Each amount is typed with two decimal places, and the column totals are added again before the sheet is signed.
- A figure that differs from the cash book by even one rupee is marked for reconciliation instead of being adjusted quietly.
- The finished statement carries the month, the office code and the initials of the person who prepared and checked it.

#CGL-DEST-002 | numeric records with dates and reference codes
- Every record in the batch begins with a reference code, followed by the date of receipt and the amount in figures.
- Dates are entered in the same day, month and year order throughout, so that sorting the list does not mix them up.
- Reference codes contain letters, slashes and digits, and each part is typed in the order printed on the source sheet.
- When two records share the same code, the operator stops and checks the source rather than guessing which one is correct.

#CGL-DEST-003 | a purchase order with item quantities
- The purchase order names each item, its unit of measure, the quantity ordered and the rate agreed with the supplier.
- Quantities are typed as whole numbers, while rates and line totals keep two decimal places.
- The line total is the quantity multiplied by the rate, and the order total is the sum of all the line totals.
- A copy of the order goes to the stores section, which uses it later to check the goods when they arrive.

#CGL-DEST-004 | invoice details and tax amounts
- An invoice is entered with its number, date, supplier name, taxable value and the tax charged at each rate.
- The tax amount is checked by applying the stated rate to the taxable value and rounding to two decimal places.
- The invoice total must equal the taxable value plus the tax; any difference is written in the remarks column.
- Invoices are entered in the order of their dates, and a missing number in a supplier's series is reported.

#CGL-DEST-005 | an employee directory with joining dates
- Each line of the directory gives an employee number, a full name, the designation, the section and the date of joining.
- Names are typed exactly as they appear in the service book, including initials and the order of the surname.
- Joining dates are entered in a single format so that the list can be sorted by length of service.
- Telephone extensions are added only when they appear on the approved list kept by the establishment section.

#CGL-DEST-006 | a postal dispatch list with tracking numbers
- The dispatch list records the serial number, the addressee, the town, the postal code and the tracking number of each article.
- Tracking numbers are long strings of letters and digits, so they are read back in groups of four after entry.
- The number of articles on the list must match the number handed over at the counter, as shown on the receipt.
- A letter returned undelivered is entered again with its original serial number and a note giving the reason.

#CGL-DEST-007 | attendance statistics by section
- Attendance is summarised for each section as the number of working days, days present, days on leave and days absent.
- The four figures for each employee must add up to the number of working days in the month.
- Section totals are calculated from the individual lines, never typed directly from an earlier report.
- Percentages are worked out to one decimal place, and the method of rounding is stated at the foot of the table.

#CGL-DEST-008 | a bank reconciliation summary for an office
- The reconciliation starts with the balance in the cash book and the balance shown in the bank statement on the same date.
- Cheques issued but not yet presented are listed with their numbers, dates and amounts.
- Deposits made late in the month that the bank has not yet credited are listed separately.
- After both adjustments, the two balances must agree exactly, and the summary is signed only when they do.

#CGL-DEST-009 | survey results presented as a numeric narrative
- The narrative turns a table of survey results into sentences, giving each figure with its unit and its base.
- Every percentage in the text is checked against the count it came from and the total number of responses.
- Large numbers are written with commas in the usual places, so that a reader can see their size at a glance.
- The narrative avoids words such as most or few unless the figures show clearly what they mean.

#CGL-DEST-010 | inventory opening and closing balances
- For each item, the inventory sheet shows the opening balance, the quantity received, the quantity issued and the closing balance.
- The closing balance is the opening balance plus receipts minus issues, and it becomes the next month's opening balance.
- A negative closing balance is impossible, so it always points to a missing receipt or a mistyped issue.
- The physical count taken at the end of the month is compared with the closing balance and any shortage is reported.

#CGL-DEST-011 | quarterly output data in tabular form
- The table shows the output of each unit for the three months of the quarter and the total for the quarter.
- The total for each unit is the sum of its three monthly figures, and the grand total is checked across and down.
- Units are listed in the same order as in the previous quarter, so that the two tables can be compared line by line.
- A sharp rise or fall in any figure is noted beside the table with the reason given by the unit.

#CGL-DEST-012 | a report containing percentages and decimal values
- The report gives figures as percentages and decimals, and each figure keeps the number of decimal places used in the source.
- A decimal point in the wrong place can change a value tenfold, so each figure is read back aloud after entry.
- Percentages that should add up to one hundred are totalled, and any small rounding difference is explained in a footnote.
- Zeros after a decimal point are typed when the source shows them, because they indicate the precision of the measurement.

#CGL-DEST-013 | consecutive reference numbers and record IDs
- A run of consecutive reference numbers is entered one per line, and the last number is checked against the first plus the count.
- A skipped or repeated number in the series is reported at once, since it may mean a lost or duplicated record.
- Record IDs keep their leading zeros, so a number such as 00417 is never shortened to 417.
- The operator types the full ID even when only the last digits change, because shortcuts lead to errors in long runs.

#CGL-DEST-014 | names, addresses and postal codes
- Each address is entered in the same order: name, house number, street, locality, town and postal code.
- Postal codes are six digits long, and an entry with five or seven digits is set aside for checking.
- Names of places are spelled as on the source document, even when another spelling is common in speech.
- Abbreviations such as Road or Street are typed in full unless the form itself uses the short form.

#CGL-DEST-015 | a schedule containing times and deadlines
- The schedule lists each task with its start date, its deadline and the officer responsible for it.
- Times are given on the twenty-four-hour clock in the working copy and in words in the circulated version.
- A deadline that falls on a holiday is moved to the next working day, and the change is shown in the notes.
- When a task depends on another, the schedule names the earlier task so that its delay can be traced.

#CGL-DEST-016 | a telephone directory verification exercise
- The directory gives each office, the officer's designation, the telephone number and the extension.
- Every number in the draft is compared with the latest list supplied by each section before the directory is printed.
- Numbers are typed in groups separated by hyphens, which makes a transposed pair of digits easier to spot.
- An entry that cannot be confirmed is marked to be verified rather than copied from an old edition.

#CGL-DEST-017 | mixed punctuation in an official data note
- The data note uses commas, semicolons, colons, brackets and quotation marks, and each one is copied exactly.
- A colon introduces a list of figures, while semicolons separate items that already contain commas.
- Brackets enclose explanations that can be skipped without changing the meaning of the sentence.
- Quotation marks are used only for the exact words of a source, never to show emphasis.

#CGL-DEST-018 | repeated words and number accuracy
- The passage repeats certain words and figures on purpose, to test whether the typist keeps an exact place.
- A typist who loses the line may type a repeated phrase twice or skip it altogether.
- Numbers that look alike, such as 1689 and 1698, appear close together and must not be swapped.
- Keeping a finger or a ruler under the source line is a simple way to avoid jumping to the wrong repetition.

#CGL-DEST-019 | a procurement summary with currency values
- The summary lists each purchase with the date, the supplier, the quantity, the value in rupees and the mode of payment.
- Rupee amounts are typed with commas for thousands and two decimal places, as in Rs. 125,000.00.
- The total value of purchases is checked against the payments recorded by the accounts section for the same period.
- Purchases above the limit set by the office are marked to show that they needed a higher approval.

#CGL-DEST-020 | a census-style household data summary
- The household summary records the number of members, their age groups, and the type of house for each household.
- Totals for each age group are added across households, and the grand total must equal the number of persons counted.
- Codes are used for the type of house, and the meaning of each code is printed beneath the table.
- No name or personal detail appears in the summary, which deals only with numbers.

#CGL-DEST-021 | a list of applications by date and status
- The list shows each application number, the date received, the subject and its present status.
- Status is recorded with one of four fixed words: received, under review, completed or returned.
- The number of applications in each status is counted at the end, and the four counts must add up to the total.
- Applications are listed in the order of their dates, oldest first, so that delays stand out.

#CGL-DEST-022 | a table-to-text conversion practice
- In this exercise, a small table is rewritten as continuous text, with every figure kept in its correct place.
- Each row of the table becomes one sentence, and the column headings become the words that introduce the figures.
- The typed text must not leave out any cell, even one that contains a zero.
- After typing, the figures in the text are ticked off against the table one by one.

#CGL-DEST-023 | a short statistical report with comparisons
- The report compares this year's figures with last year's and gives the change as a number and as a percentage.
- A rise is described as an increase and a fall as a decrease, and the base year is named every time.
- Small changes are reported plainly, without words that suggest more significance than the figures support.
- The table at the end repeats every figure used in the text, so that a reader can check each comparison.

#CGL-DEST-024 | a high-density key-depression passage with figures
- The passage is packed with numbers, codes and punctuation, so that nearly every word needs a shift or a digit key.
- Speed in such a passage comes from a steady rhythm rather than bursts followed by long pauses.
- A typist who looks at the keyboard for each digit loses time, so the number row is practised separately beforehand.
- Errors in figures are counted strictly, since a wrong digit makes a record useless.

#CGL-DEST-025 | a fifteen-minute data-entry practice session
- The practice session uses a long sheet of records, each with a code, a date, a quantity and an amount.
- The operator keeps a steady pace and does not stop to correct earlier lines unless the method allows it.
- At the end of the session, the number of records entered and the number of mistakes are noted separately.
- The aim of the practice is a high count of correct key depressions, not a high count of hurried ones.

// ---------------------------------------------------------------- AIIMS-CRE
#AIIMS-CRE-001 | the daily service summary of a patient registration desk
- The summary gives the number of new registrations, repeat visits and enquiries handled at the desk during the day.
- New registrations are counted from the serial numbers issued, not from memory or a rough tally.
- Busy hours are noted in the summary, which helps the office plan the number of counters for the next week.
- Any registration cancelled during the day is shown with its number and the reason, so the count stays accurate.

#AIIMS-CRE-002 | appointment scheduling and queue management
- Appointments are booked in fixed slots, and each slot shows the patient's registration number and the clinic.
- A patient who arrives without an appointment is given the next free slot and told the expected waiting time.
- The queue display is updated as each patient is called, so that people waiting can see their turn approaching.
- Slots left empty by patients who do not arrive are released for others after a short waiting period.

#AIIMS-CRE-003 | medical record file numbering and retrieval
- Each medical record file carries a number made up of the year of registration and a running serial.
- Files are shelved in numerical order, and a tracer card marks the place of any file taken out.
- When a file is requested, the clerk notes the name of the requesting department and the time it left the shelf.
- A file not returned within the agreed time is followed up with the department that borrowed it.

#AIIMS-CRE-004 | a hospital admission and discharge register
- The register records the patient's number, the ward, the date and time of admission and the date and time of discharge.
- Each admission line is completed at the time of entry, and the discharge columns are filled only when the patient leaves.
- The number of patients in each ward at midnight is worked out from the register and reported the next morning.
- Corrections in the register are made with a single line through the wrong entry and the initials of the person correcting it.

#AIIMS-CRE-005 | outpatient department attendance statistics
- The statistics show the number of patients seen in each clinic, divided into new and follow-up visits.
- Daily figures are added to give weekly and monthly totals, which are compared with the same period last year.
- A clinic that is closed on a particular day is shown with a dash rather than a zero, to avoid confusion.
- The figures are typed into a standard table so that the department heads can read them quickly.

#AIIMS-CRE-006 | an inventory of medical and office supplies
- The inventory lists each item with its code, the unit, the opening stock, the receipts, the issues and the closing stock.
- Items with an expiry date are listed with that date, and stock nearing expiry is issued first.
- The reorder level of each item is printed beside it, and any item below that level is highlighted for purchase.
- The store keeper and a second staff member sign the monthly count to confirm that it is correct.

#AIIMS-CRE-007 | a laboratory report dispatch register
- The register records the report number, the patient's registration number, the test, and the department to which the report was sent.
- Each report is entered before it leaves the laboratory office, together with the time of dispatch.
- The receiving department signs the register, which proves that the report reached the right place.
- Reports collected by patients in person are entered with the time of collection and the patient's signature.

#AIIMS-CRE-008 | referral note routing between departments
- A referral note names the referring doctor, the department to which the patient is referred, and the reason in brief.
- The clerk enters the note in the referral register and gives the patient a slip with the room number and the time.
- Urgent referrals are marked clearly and sent ahead so that the receiving department expects the patient.
- When the patient is seen, the receiving department records the date, which closes the entry in the register.

#AIIMS-CRE-009 | a patient helpdesk giving directions and information
- The helpdesk answers questions about clinic timings, room numbers, registration and the location of facilities.
- Answers are given from an up-to-date information sheet rather than from memory, which may be out of date.
- Questions that the helpdesk cannot answer are written down and passed to the right department with a promise of a reply.
- The helpdesk keeps a count of the questions asked, which shows where better signs or notices are needed.

#AIIMS-CRE-010 | a hospital cleaning and infection-control notice
- The notice sets out the times at which wards, corridors and waiting areas are cleaned each day.
- It asks visitors to use the hand-washing points placed at the entrance of every ward.
- Areas being cleaned are marked with a sign, and visitors are asked to wait until the floor is dry.
- The notice is typed in short, clear sentences and printed in large letters so that it can be read from a distance.

#AIIMS-CRE-011 | medicine store stock reconciliation
- Reconciliation compares the stock shown in the store ledger with the stock actually counted on the shelves.
- Each medicine is counted by batch, and the batch number and expiry date are checked against the ledger.
- A difference between the ledger and the count is investigated before any adjustment is entered.
- Expired stock is recorded separately and kept apart until it is disposed of under the store's procedure.

#AIIMS-CRE-012 | an ambulance movement and service log
- The log records each trip with the vehicle number, the time of leaving, the destination and the time of return.
- The odometer reading is noted at the start and end of every trip, and the distance is worked out from the two.
- The driver signs each entry, and the supervisor checks the log at the end of every shift.
- Fuel taken during the day is entered with the quantity and the receipt number.

#AIIMS-CRE-013 | health camp registration and follow-up
- Each person attending the camp is registered with a camp number, age, and the service received.
- The registration sheet is typed into the main record within two days, so that follow-up can be planned.
- People asked to return for a check are listed with the date of their follow-up visit.
- The camp report gives the total attendance and the number of people referred to the hospital.

#AIIMS-CRE-014 | confidential handling of patient information
- Patient information is shared only with staff who need it for the patient's care or for an approved office task.
- Files and screens are never left open where visitors can read them, even for a short time.
- Requests for copies of records are entered in a register and dealt with only after the request is approved.
- Staff speak about a patient's details only in private, never at the counter where others can hear.

#AIIMS-CRE-015 | preparing an administrative meeting agenda
- The agenda lists the date, time and place of the meeting, followed by the items in the order they will be taken up.
- Each item names the officer who will present it and any paper that members should read beforehand.
- The agenda is sent to members several days ahead, with the papers attached in the same order as the items.
- Items carried over from the previous meeting are listed first, so that pending matters are not forgotten.

#AIIMS-CRE-016 | drafting a formal hospital office letter
- A formal letter carries a reference number, the date, the name and address of the recipient, a subject line and a salutation.
- The first paragraph states the purpose of the letter, and the following paragraphs give the details in order.
- The letter ends with a clear request or statement of what will happen next, followed by the signature block.
- A copy of every letter is kept in the file, and its reference number is entered in the dispatch register.

#AIIMS-CRE-017 | a short report on waiting times
- The report measures the time between registration and consultation for a sample of patients in each clinic.
- Waiting times are given as an average and as the longest wait, because an average alone can hide long delays.
- The report compares the figures for mornings and afternoons, which often differ.
- Its recommendations are practical, such as opening an extra counter at the busiest hour.

#AIIMS-CRE-018 | a public health awareness essay
- The essay explains, in plain language, how simple habits such as washing hands and drinking clean water prevent illness.
- It avoids medical terms that ordinary readers may not know, or explains them in a few words when they are needed.
- The essay encourages readers to visit a health centre early rather than waiting until an illness becomes serious.
- Each paragraph makes one point, which keeps the essay easy to follow when it is read aloud.

#AIIMS-CRE-019 | a letter about improving patient services
- The letter thanks patients for their suggestions and explains which of them the hospital has been able to act on.
- It describes each improvement in a sentence, such as longer counter hours or clearer signs.
- Where a suggestion could not be adopted, the letter gives the reason politely.
- The letter invites further suggestions and gives the address to which they can be sent.

#AIIMS-CRE-020 | data accuracy in medical record entry
- Each entry is typed from the original form, and the patient's number is checked before any other field is filled.
- Dates, names and test results are read back after entry, because an error in any of them can affect care.
- Fields that are blank on the form are left blank in the system, never filled with a guess.
- At the end of each batch, a second person checks a sample of entries against the forms.

#AIIMS-CRE-021 | a staff duty roster and shift handover
- The roster shows each staff member's name, designation and shift for every day of the week.
- Changes to the roster are made only with the approval of the officer in charge and are recorded with the date.
- At each handover, the outgoing staff member notes pending tasks in the handover book.
- The incoming staff member reads and signs the note, so that responsibility passes clearly from one shift to the next.

#AIIMS-CRE-022 | an equipment maintenance request register
- Each request names the equipment, its asset number, the department, the fault reported and the date of the request.
- The engineering section enters the date on which it attended and the action taken.
- A request remains open until the department confirms that the equipment is working again.
- The register is reviewed every week, and requests open for a long time are brought to the notice of the supervisor.

#AIIMS-CRE-023 | arrangements for a blood donation camp
- The arrangements cover the venue, the date, the time, the staff on duty and the materials needed.
- Donors are registered at a separate counter, and each is given a number that follows them through the camp.
- Refreshments and a resting area are arranged for donors after they have given blood.
- The summary report gives the number of donors registered and the number who were able to donate.

#AIIMS-CRE-024 | a noting and drafting scenario for a computer skill test
- In noting, the clerk summarises a case in a few paragraphs and ends with a clear point for the officer to decide.
- In drafting, the clerk prepares the letter or order that will carry out the officer's decision.
- Both the note and the draft use short sentences, correct names and accurate dates.
- The draft is formatted with a heading, numbered paragraphs and a signature block, as the office style requires.

#AIIMS-CRE-025 | a typing passage on healthcare office administration
- Healthcare offices depend on accurate records, because every patient visit creates documents that others will rely on.
- The registration desk, the records department and the stores all follow written procedures for their daily work.
- Staff in these offices balance speed with care, since a hurried entry may cause a delay later.
- A clear and polite manner at the counter is as important as accurate typing in the back office.
